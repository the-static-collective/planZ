# COMMON TABLE / WASECA — GRAND PLAN DRAFT 001

**planZ proposal · 2026-10-21 · NOT CANON · NOT A PARTNER COMMITMENT**

**Working names:** The Common Table; Waseca Commons; Jubilee Commons (none adopted by a grocery organization or other partner).

**Core proposition:** Establish a human-first, community-governed way to connect locally produced food, hospitality, art, youth creativity, practical skills, and neighbors' voluntary contributions. Begin with a small card-routing experiment at two upcoming gatherings; earn the right to test one pop-up; evaluate whether a permanent multi-use commons is warranted. The *Waseca community grocery initiative is a particular possible collaborator, not an acquired project or an approved host.*

> **Get the right card into the right hands.** The primary game action is noticing somebody else's capabilities. Nobody becomes a volunteer, partner, customer, beneficiary, or participant in a database merely by entering a room or receiving a card.

## 1. North star: one table, five independently viable doors

| Door | Human-scale experience | Necessary owner/operational proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Market** | Local grocery/produce, affordable necessities, producers visible | Grocery operator or existing retailer owns buying, inventory, POS, safe custody and pricing. |
| **Kitchen / Café** | Farm-to-table service, coffee, meals, cooking education | Licensed food operator; separate food-safety, staffing, waste and equipment plan. |
| **Stage** | Live local music, youth performance, spoken word | Venue host and artists agree terms; all-ages consent, sound, fire code and scheduling checked. |
| **Gallery / Maker Wall** | Display and sale of work by local creators | Consignment terms, liability, attribution, returns, sales tax and payout process set. |
| **Commons / Workshop** | Skills, repair, classes, learning, card game, local introductions | Explicit local steward, accessible events, scope of care, safety and voluntary participation. |

These can share one building **later** or be tested at different existing venues **now**. The grocery need not finance the other doors. A permanent property is an option to evaluate, not a precondition.

**User experience:** Come for groceries; stay for a meal; discover a performance or artwork; meet a neighbor; find an opportunity; take a card; return if you choose.

## 2. The Right Hands game: POSTYMON × REALITY.2

- **POSTYMON / What exists:** discoverable capabilities and resources (the grower, cook, cold chain, stage, youth mentor, wall, route, keeper, etc.).
- **REALITY.2 / What could become:** bounded opportunities or questions (find a kitchen partner; identify a route; host one listening night; explore fair artist settlement).
- **Victory condition:** a card/question reaches someone better positioned to respond **or** returns with a useful, attributable 'already solved / not appropriate / no match'. Completing work is not required for a successful pass.
- **Moves:** notice → hold → offer to pass → accept or decline → ask → optionally propose a next action → return a bounded answer. Passing is voluntary; receipt is not a commitment.
- **Missing card rule:** a person may name a missing need or capability and suggest a new card; additions remain provisional until reviewed by the locally accountable host.
- **No attendee tracking:** do not log the identities or movements of casual card holders. For first field use, paper return slips and synthetic/offline records only. Actual contact exchange occurs separately by mutual permission.

### Proposed minimal event model (not an adopted technical contract)

`card.created` / `card.proposed` / `recognition.noted` / `handoff.offered` / `handoff.accepted|declined` / `action.proposed` / `action.accepted` / `fulfillment.reported` / `fulfillment.confirmed|disputed|withdrawn`.

Treat **recognition**, **proposal**, **agreement**, **report**, and **confirmation** as different claims. Never make a scanned QR code an implicit acceptance or assert that a signed log proves physical performance.

For Saturday, aim for 3 useful questions and 1-3 consensual introductions, **not** a count of 'recruited people'.

## 3. Operating loops — deliberately separate

**Food custody:** grower/producer → approved receiving party → check & inventory → storage → customer/recipient → returns, spoilage and reconciliation. Ask who has the authority, thermometer, keys, insurance, records and duty to stop.

**Creative economy:** creator offers work or a performance → agreement specifying ownership and compensation → audience purchases/tips only through an authorized arrangement → gross, fees, taxes and agreed deductions recorded → creator payout + acknowledgement. Start with **direct-to-artist** methods when possible. A venue collecting and distributing funds for creators requires documented processor terms, tax, wage/worker classification, financial custody and reconciliation review. Youth payment and participation require guardian/safeguarding consideration.

**Mutual aid / PENNY:** local steward receives an explicit, bounded need → review/permission → offer → independent acceptance → activity report → recipient-side confirmation or unresolved result. No requirement to attend worship, appear on a broadcast, donate, join a co-op or use an account to get help.

**Do not net these together:** 1. commercial store receipts; 2. charitable contributions and aid; 3. nonmonetary work, introductions and gifts. A penny, a volunteer hour, and a commercial dollar are not an invented universal token. Co-op equity, if relevant, remains under its governing instruments.

## 4. Saturday Oct 24 → Sunday Oct 25: two distinct conversations

### Saturday · Coalition of Care: discover, do not recruit

One proposed Open Space / Pro Action Café prompt:

> **Could local food, creative space and practical community skills become easier to connect by giving people small cards that help them recognize one another's capabilities?**

Bring: 24-card POSTYMON × REALITY.2 paper deck; 5–10 one-page invitations; unconnected offline HTML demonstration if invited; blank anonymous return slips; a pocket notebook for **non-identifying** observations.

Ask each participant: *Which card reminds you of somebody? What would you need to know before making an introduction? What would make this safe and worthwhile?*

Three high-value roles to encounter (not targets to claim): a local grower/supply-chain operator, a community food-access or food-safety practitioner, and an arts/youth/venue steward. A conversation can end with a helpful refusal.

**Exit criteria:** a few genuinely useful, permission-based next questions; a short harvest sheet distinguishing observations from offers; no unsolicited participant database or public service signups.

### Sunday · Waseca community grocery planning: listen for the governing particular

Do **not** claim to represent, name, or plan the grocery. Ask:

1. What is the committee proving next—membership, demand, governance, capital, location, suppliers, or operations?
2. Would a future multi-use community room support or distract from that mission?
3. Which parties have authority over premises, operations, partners and public communications?
4. What existing regional relationships are already working?
5. Would an independent, fictional 10-minute card/logistics trial help, and who could properly authorize a later real pilot?

Offer **one** bounded experiment if invited: return a contact-free discovery summary, rehearse one paper food handoff, or convene a small food-and-arts pilot planning call. Don't conflate offering with approval.

### Pilot budget and feasibility worksheet (fill with quotes, not guesses)

Fixed: venue rental, utilities, permits, event insurance, staff/security, sound, accessible setup, cleaning.

Variable: ingredients, food labor, packaging, payment fees, waste, artist guarantees/payouts, printing, refunds, transport.

Revenue categories: lawful food sales; art commissions; ticketed entry only if compatible with accessible participation; direct tips; separately authorized donations or sponsorships. **Never treat optional gifts as guaranteed operating income.**

Questions: Can the event cover costs without extracting free labor? What capacity is needed? Does it improve affordable food access? Can artists see net payment statements? Would it still work if software failed?

## 6. Architecture: possible adapters, not automatic integrations

```text
PAPER CARDS / HUMAN CONVERSATIONS
            |
  optional Abundent public card page
            |
   human-review / local consent
            |
  GHoT-style offer and routing logic
         /       \
    reLATTE     PENNY lane
  compatibility  bounded voluntary-help receipts
         \       /
     local operator systems
   (food POS, finance, arts payout,
    venue schedule remain authoritative)
```

- **Abundent:** potential public *return address* for a card, print-at-home deck, event pages, opt-in public-safe questions. Do not publish an unverified QR or collect personal details by default.
- **GHoT:** model selective delegation and voluntary handoff. It cannot appoint people, volunteers, employers, or stewards simply because a graph matched.
- **reLATTE:** possible transfer/compatibility contract preserving provenance and local authority; not proof of a live integration.
- **PENNY / Jubilee:** contribution records and non-coercive help claims; not a bank, speculative token, payment custodian or replacement for store ledgers.
- **planZ:** preserve this as a proposal, track variants and counterevidence, and defer all operational authority to the institutions and people actually responsible.

**Smallest useful software:** a static printable card catalog with stable non-personal IDs, explanatory consent states, opt-in return notes, export/import and archival/version lineage. An offline page already exists in Drive. A multi-user service, payments processor integration, dispatch system, or public needs intake **must not** be implied by that prototype.

## 7. Stewardship and safeguarding gates

Before any public launch: permission from venue and relevant organizer; privacy notice and data-minimization review; accessibility and language accommodation; appropriate food and premises licensing; fire/building/occupancy rules; minors' participation, supervision, media and compensation permissions; insurance; performer/artist agreements; money handling, refund and tax review; conflicts and complaints pathway; named person able to suspend the experiment.

No special status for people who use cards. No opt-out penalty. No transfer of faith, affiliation, beneficiary histories or relationship maps into a commercial profile. Identifiable stories and contacts stay with the human or organization explicitly authorized to hold them.

## 8. Evidence, metrics, and stop conditions

Track separately:

- **Discovery:** specific non-identifying unknowns answered; number of *consensual* introductions, without logging casual handoffs.
- **Pilot execution:** completed events vs announced events; steward minutes; food safely received/sold; spoiled/returned units; resolved/unresolved receipt disputes.
- **Artist outcomes:** gross receipts, fees and net payouts; payout timing; creator understanding/approval; optional qualitative response.
- **Human benefit:** attendee usefulness, accessibility, willingness to return, genuine food-affordability relevance and burden on partners.
- **Risk:** incidents, privacy leaks, unapproved commitments, unpaid guaranteed work, out-of-scope money custody.

Stop, revise or suspend if a host declines; a child-safety or food-safety gate is not met; payments cannot be reconciled; people feel pressured into participation; or coordination creates more burdens than value.

**Do not use:** card distributions as proof of consent; attendance as evidence of enrollment; promises as work receipts; a nice narrative as business feasibility; introductions as partnerships.
